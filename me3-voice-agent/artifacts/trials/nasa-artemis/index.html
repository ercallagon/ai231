NASA Shares Plans to Accelerate Artemis, Return to the Moon

On October 23, 2026, NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman and other agency leaders updated the path to returning Americans to the lunar surface in 2028.

Summary

NASA provided updates on previously announced acceleration plans for the Artemis program, advancing toward the Artemis III test flight in 2027 and the Artemis IV Moon landing mission in 2028 [1].

Artemis III: Earth-orbit test (2027)

  • Four astronauts will fly aboard Orion on NASA's SLS rocket in a planned 14-day mission in Earth orbit [1].
  • The mission will test rendezvous, docking, and operations between Orion and lunar lander test vehicles from SpaceX and Blue Origin in Earth orbit [1].
  • The mission's purpose is to reduce risk ahead of the Artemis IV Moon landing [1].
  • NASA reports that hardware for Artemis III is on track, with processing timelines sharply reduced by about half [1].

Artemis IV: Moon landing (2028)

  • Astronauts will launch aboard Orion on the SLS rocket from NASA's Kennedy Space Center in Florida [1].
  • For Artemis IV, NASA is pursuing two upper-stage options for SLS: its current interim cryogenic propulsion stage, and a stretched version of ULA's Centaur V that can carry more propellant [1].
  • NASA is pursuing multiple paths to a Moon landing, and NASA says it will send Artemis astronauts on increasingly difficult missions at the lunar South Pole [1].

Leadership quote

"NASA is driving outcomes to make Moon missions a reality, and we're not waiting for others to bring their plans to us — the campaign must move with urgency as strategic competitors pursue a permanent presence beyond Earth."
NASA, quoted in the October 23, 2026 update [1]
